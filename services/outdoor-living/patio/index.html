Finishes

Four finishes, four different patios

The finish decides how the patio looks, how it feels underfoot and how much upkeep it needs. Pick one to see where it fits best.

Broom finish

Standard broom finish

A broom drawn across wet concrete leaves a textured, slip-resistant surface, the most practical choice for everyday use.

Pick My Finish
  • Slip-resistant texture
  • Drains well
  • Handles heavy foot traffic
  • Minimal maintenance
Exposed aggregate

Exposed aggregate

The surface paste is washed off while the concrete is fresh, revealing the natural stone or gravel in the mix.

Pick My Finish
  • Natural color variation
  • Non-slip texture
  • Holds up in direct sun
  • Resists fading
Stamped

Stamped concrete

Patterns pressed in before the concrete cures imitate brick, slate, cobblestone or tile, with color release for depth.

Pick My Finish
  • Brick or slate patterns
  • Cobblestone or tile looks
  • Color release for contrast
  • Reseal every two to three years
Borders

Decorative borders

A contrasting border around a standard or aggregate slab defines the edges without the cost of stamping the whole surface.

Pick My Finish
  • Stamped or formed edge
  • Pairs with broom or aggregate
  • Frames the patio
  • Ties into fencing or landscaping

Louisiana ground

Built for clay soil and heavy rain

Skipping or rushing the sub-base is the most common reason concrete patios fail early here. This is what we build against.

Expansive clay

Clay soil swells and shrinks as moisture changes, stressing the slab from below.

Compacted sub-base

Gravel or crushed stone is compacted in lifts to give the slab stable, draining support.

Steel reinforcement

Rebar or welded wire mesh holds the slab together and reduces cracking as the ground shifts.

Grading for runoff

Drainage problems get solved at the grading stage, before the sub-base goes down.

Do you need a permit?

Ground-level slabs typically do not need a permit in most parishes. A patio that connects to the house or changes drainage may. We confirm it at the site visit.

Source: our live patio installation page. Permit rules vary by parish and by project.

How it works

Six stages, none of them rushed

Every patio follows the same sequence from the first visit to the final seal.

  1. 01

    Consultation and site review

    We meet on site to check drainage and grading, talk through layout and show you the available finishes.

  2. 02

    Written estimate

    You get the scope, materials, finish and timeline in writing, and nothing gets added after work starts that was not in it.

  3. 03

    Site preparation

    We excavate, grade the area to slope water away from the house, compact it and lay the gravel sub-base.

  4. 04

    Forms and reinforcement

    Wooden forms set the shape, and rebar or welded wire mesh goes inside before the pour.

  5. 05

    Placement and finishing

    The concrete is placed, screeded level and finished. For stamped work, patterns and color go in before it sets.

  6. 06

    Curing and sealing

    Light foot traffic is usually fine after 24 to 48 hours. Once curing is done, we apply the sealer.

Cost

What drives the cost of a new patio

No two yards are the same, so we price from an on-site visit. These five things shape the number.

1

Patio size

A larger slab takes more concrete and more labor time.

2

Finish type

Stamping needs more materials, tools and labor than a broom finish.

3

Site conditions

Drainage problems or heavy grading add preparation work.

4

Old concrete removal

Breaking out and hauling away an existing slab adds to the scope.

5

Add-on features

Steps, borders, walkway connections or prep for a future pergola or kitchen change the total.

Every estimate is itemized and written before we break ground.

Questions

Patio installation questions

Timelines, permits, soil and upkeep, answered plainly.

How long does patio installation take?

Most residential patios are finished in two to four days. The slab then needs 24 to 48 hours before light foot traffic and reaches full strength at 28 days.

Do I need a permit to install a patio in New Orleans?

Ground-level concrete slabs typically do not need a permit in most parishes. Projects that connect to the home's structure or change drainage may, and we confirm the requirements during the site visit.

How does Louisiana clay soil affect a concrete patio?

Clay expands and contracts as moisture changes, which stresses the slab from below. Proper sub-base compaction, drainage grading and rebar reinforcement offset that movement and extend the slab's life.

Can the patio connect to my walkway or driveway?

Yes. Transitions to existing concrete, including steps and border connections, can be part of the same project.

Is stamped concrete slippery when wet?

Smooth stamped surfaces can get slippery in rain. A non-slip sealer additive improves traction and is worth discussing when you choose a finish.

How often does a concrete patio need resealing?

Most sealers last two to three years in the New Orleans climate. Reseal when water stops beading on the surface.

Can you build a patio in a yard with drainage problems?

Yes, but the drainage has to be fixed at the grading stage before the sub-base goes down. We evaluate it during the site assessment and grade to suit.
