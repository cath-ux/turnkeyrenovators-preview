Climate and rules

Siding that works in this climate

Intense heat, heavy rain and hurricanes are hard on exterior walls. Good siding keeps water out and wind off the structure.

Storms and wind

Quality siding blocks harsh winds and keeps storm water from getting into the walls.

Moisture and mold

Water that gets behind weak siding leads to mold and structural damage, so installation methods and materials must resist moisture.

Heat

Materials with good thermal properties help a home stay comfortable through long, hot and humid summers.

Historic districts

Outside the French Quarter, the Historic District Landmarks Commission reviews exterior changes to homes in local historic districts, and new siding counts as one.

Historic review steps come from the City of New Orleans HDLC. We check your address during the consultation.

Questions

Siding questions

Signs, timelines and climate, filtered by topic.

What are the signs that my siding needs repair or replacement?

Cracks, warping, rotting, fading, peeling paint or rising energy bills from poor insulation. If you notice any of these, have a professional inspect the siding to see how far the damage goes.

Which siding materials do you install?

Vinyl, wood and fiber cement. Vinyl is the low-maintenance option, wood gives a classic natural look, and fiber cement adds strong resistance to moisture, termites and fire.

How long does a siding installation take in New Orleans?

It depends on the size of your home, the material, the weather and the complexity of the job. Most installations take anywhere from a few days to a couple of weeks.

Can I install siding myself?

Some experienced homeowners do, but larger or more complex jobs are best left to a professional. Proper installation prevents problems and helps the siding last.

Are there specific considerations for siding in the New Orleans climate?

Yes. Our humid subtropical climate calls for installation techniques and materials that resist moisture and mold, and materials with good thermal properties help in hot, humid conditions.

Do I need approval for new siding in a historic district?

Usually. Exterior work on a property in a local historic district goes through the Historic District Landmarks Commission, and you must apply for a building permit to start that process. We check your address during the consultation.
