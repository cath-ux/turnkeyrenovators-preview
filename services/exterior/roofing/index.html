Weather and permits

What a New Orleans roof has to survive

Heat, humidity, sudden storms and historic district rules all shape how a roof here should be planned and built.

Hurricane season

Strong winds and heavy rain make hurricane-resistant materials the starting point for any roof in the city.

Heat, humidity and salt air

Intense heat and humidity shorten roof life and salt air adds corrosion risk, so we choose materials that resist mold and mildew.

City permits

Planning covers the permits your roof needs and any neighborhood association restrictions before work starts.

Historic districts

Outside the French Quarter, exterior work in a local historic district goes through the Historic District Landmarks Commission, starting with a building permit application.

Planning around the season

Where we can, we avoid starting roof projects during hurricane season so weather delays stay to a minimum.

Source for historic review: the City of New Orleans HDLC. We confirm what your address needs when we inspect the roof.

Questions

Roofing questions

What New Orleans homeowners ask us most about materials, timing and warranties.

What roofing materials hold up best in New Orleans?

Hurricane-resistant materials come first. Metal and slate handle intense sun and harsh weather well, and in our humidity we favor materials that resist mold and mildew.

Can you keep the historic look of my roof?

Yes. On historic homes we use period-appropriate materials such as slate or ceramic tile, keep original features where we can, and add modern insulation that does not change how the roof looks.

When is the best time to start a roof project?

Outside hurricane season when possible, which keeps weather delays down. Planning also covers your timeline, budget, city permits and any neighborhood association restrictions.

What are the signs my roof needs work?

Frequent leaks and missing shingles are the clearest signals. A jump in energy bills can also point to poor insulation that calls for a repair or a replacement.

How often should my roof be inspected?

Regularly, and always after severe weather. Inspections uncover hidden damage early, before it turns into a bigger repair.

Do you work on commercial roofs?

Yes. We plan commercial roofing around local zoning and building codes and schedule repairs to cause as little disruption to your business as possible.

What warranties come with a new roof?

Our roof replacements carry a GAF 50-year non pro-rated material warranty, a 10-year workmanship warranty and a 15-year unlimited wind warranty that covers named storms.
