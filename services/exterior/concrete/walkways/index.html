Finish options

Five finishes for foot traffic

A walkway gets wet and gets walked on daily, so grip comes first. Here is how each finish stacks up.

Broom and exposed aggregate

Brushed lines or revealed stone give reliable traction in wet weather.

  • Broom is most popular
  • Aggregate has excellent grip
  • Both suit rainy weather

Stamped and colored

Patterns and integral color deliver a paver look without laying individual pavers.

  • Brick, flagstone, slate, cobblestone
  • Color mixed in before pouring
  • Pairs with broom or stamp

Smooth trowel

A flat, polished surface that is slippery when wet, so we keep it to covered walks and accents.

  • Covered walkways
  • Decorative accents
  • Not for open walks
Accessibility

What an accessible walk has to meet

Commercial walkways on an accessible route follow the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design. These are the numbers we build to.

Clear width

Walking surfaces must be at least 36 inches wide, with only short pinch points allowed down to 32 inches.

Slope

Running slope can be no steeper than 1:20, and cross slope no steeper than 1:48. A running slope steeper than 1:20 is treated as a ramp.

Surface

Floor and ground surfaces must be stable, firm and slip resistant, so we pair accessible routes with a textured finish.

Detectable warnings

Where a detectable warning surface is required, its truncated domes must measure 0.9 to 1.4 inches across at the base.

Working in the public right-of-way?

Sidewalk work in the right-of-way may need a permit in New Orleans proper, and requirements vary in Jefferson Parish and nearby areas. We identify what your project needs.

How we build it

Eight steps on every path

The same sequence on every job, so nothing gets missed between layout and cure.

  1. 01

    Assessment and layout

    We check soil, tree roots and drainage, then set the best route for the path.

  2. 02

    Old surface removal

    Existing concrete is broken up and hauled away down to soil level.

  3. 03

    Grading and sub-base

    We set the slope for runoff and compact the ground into a stable base.

  4. 04

    Forms

    Wood or metal forms define width and edges, with flexible forms for smooth curves.

  5. 05

    Reinforcement

    Rebar, wire mesh or fiber resists cracking from soil movement and temperature swings.

  6. 06

    Pour and finish

    Concrete is spread evenly and finished to your texture, with edges tooled to a rounded profile.

  7. 07

    Control joints

    Joints cut at regular intervals keep any cracks on planned lines.

  8. 08

    Curing

    Stay off the walk 24 to 48 hours for light foot traffic and up to 7 days before full use.

Cost

What affects the cost of a walkway

We price after the site assessment. These five factors move the number.

1

Size and length

A simple 30-foot front walk costs less than a network of paths connecting several areas.

2

Site conditions

Old walkway removal, tree root work and tight equipment access add scope.

3

Finish type

Broom is the most affordable. Aggregate, stamping and color cost more.

4

Reinforcement and drainage

Extra reinforcement near large trees or on problem soil is built into the estimate.

5

ADA compliance

Wider paths, controlled slopes and detectable warnings add material and labor on commercial jobs.

The estimate comes after we see the site, so there are no surprises once work starts.

Questions

Walkway questions

Size, timing, cracks and commercial work, sorted by topic.

How wide should a residential concrete walkway be?

Most front walks are 3 to 4 feet wide, which fits one person comfortably or two side by side at 4 feet. Side-yard utility paths can be 2 to 3 feet. We size it to how the path is actually used.

Do I need a permit for a concrete walkway in New Orleans?

It depends on location and scope. Work in the public right-of-way may need a permit in New Orleans proper, and requirements vary in Jefferson Parish and surrounding areas. We help identify what your project needs.

How long does it take to install a new walkway?

Most residential projects take 2 to 4 days, depending on length and whether old concrete comes out. Allow 24 to 48 hours before light foot traffic, and full cure reaches optimal strength at about 28 days.

Can you build a curved walkway?

Yes. Curves are common on front walks and garden paths, and flexible forms keep the width and thickness consistent through every arc.

What causes concrete walkways to crack in New Orleans?

Expansive clay soil, tree roots under the slab, poor sub-base compaction and control joints spaced too far apart. Proper preparation and reinforcement at install time cut these risks significantly.

Can you match new concrete to my existing walkway?

We match thickness, grade and finish type as closely as possible. New concrete looks lighter than aged concrete at first, and weathering evens out the difference over several months.

Do you handle walkways for commercial and multi-family properties?

Yes. We install and repair walks for commercial buildings, apartment complexes and townhome communities, including ADA compliance, heavier traffic and coordination with property managers.

How thick should a concrete walkway be?

Standard residential walkways are poured at 4 inches. Paths that carry heavier loads such as lawn equipment may benefit from 5 inches, and we assess the intended use during the site visit.
