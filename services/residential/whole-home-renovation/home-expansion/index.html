Zoning, flood and code

What your lot will actually allow

Setbacks, open space, flood rules and building code decide the size of an addition before the budget does. We check all of them in the feasibility assessment.

Setbacks

In the historic urban neighborhood districts, interior side yards run 3 feet in the two-family districts, and building height tops out at 35 feet, or 40 in the Garden District.

Permeable open space

Those districts keep at least 30 percent of the lot permeable, so new footprint and new paving both count against an addition.

Substantial improvement

Under flood program rules, work costing half or more of the building's pre-project market value can require the whole house to meet new-construction standards, including elevation.

Permits and plans

New Orleans asks for a plot plan with existing and proposed square footage, plus stamped plans from an architect or civil engineer, for every addition.

New bedrooms need an escape opening

Building code requires an emergency escape and rescue opening in each new sleeping room of an addition, so we size bedroom windows for it from the start.

Sources: City of New Orleans Guide to Building Permits, the International Residential Code as adopted in Louisiana, and our own guide to expanding a New Orleans home. Your district and lot set the exact numbers.

How it works

From lot survey to final inspection

The same steps cover new rooms and expanded ones.

  1. 01

    Feasibility assessment

    We evaluate your lot, your home's structure, zoning requirements and the spaces you want to add or enlarge.

  2. 02

    Custom design

    Plans show exactly how new rooms fit or how existing ones grow, and how both connect to your current layout.

  3. 03

    Engineering and permits

    We handle structural engineering, permit applications and any required historic district review.

  4. 04

    Foundation

    Additions need new foundations. Expansions may need foundation extensions or changes. We manage the groundwork for both.

  5. 05

    Framing and exterior tie-in

    New framing ties into your existing walls, roof and siding with no awkward transitions or mismatched lines.

  6. 06

    Systems

    Electrical, plumbing and HVAC are extended to serve the new rooms or upgraded to carry the larger ones.

  7. 07

    Interior finishes

    Drywall, flooring, trim, paint, fixtures and cabinetry are finished to match or complement the rest of the house.

  8. 08

    Inspection and walkthrough

    We confirm everything meets code and works, then walk the new space with you.

Cost

What sets the price of an addition

We price your project after we see it, not from a generic cost per square foot. These factors drive the number.

1

Project type

New additions usually cost more per square foot than expansions, because they need new foundation, walls and roof.

2

Size and scope

A 100 square foot kitchen bump-out costs far less than a 1,200 square foot second story with several rooms.

3

Foundation

Soil conditions, the existing foundation type and whether we extend or build new all affect cost.

4

Structural complexity

Second stories, large cantilevers and major roof changes need more engineering and specialized labor.

5

Systems and finishes

New or upgraded electrical, plumbing and HVAC add scope, and finish level sets a wide range on its own.

6

Permits and reviews

Historic districts and some neighborhoods need extra approvals that add time and fees.

After the consultation, you get a detailed proposal with clear pricing and options, so you know exactly what you are getting before work begins.

Questions

Addition and expansion questions

Permits, structure and timing, sorted by topic.

What is the difference between a home addition and an expansion?

An addition builds entirely new space, with new walls, foundation and roof. An expansion enlarges existing rooms by pushing out walls or absorbing nearby areas. Many projects do both.

How long does a home addition or expansion take?

A simple bump-out might take six to eight weeks. A full bedroom and bathroom addition usually takes three to five months, and second stories or large combined projects can take six months or longer.

Do I need permits for an addition or expansion in New Orleans?

Yes. Both need building permits, and most also need electrical, plumbing and mechanical permits. Projects in historic districts need additional review. We handle the whole permitting process for you.

Can my foundation support a second-story addition?

It depends on the foundation type and condition. We evaluate it at the first consultation and bring in structural engineers as needed. Some foundations carry a second story as built, others need reinforcement first.

How much space can I add to my property?

That depends on lot size, existing lot coverage, setbacks, height limits and zoning. We check all of these in the feasibility assessment and design the most space your lot allows within code.

Is it cheaper to expand a room or add a new one?

Expansions usually cost less per square foot because they extend existing systems instead of building new structure. Sometimes a new addition still makes more sense for how you live, even at a higher cost.

Do I need to move out during construction?

It depends on where the work happens. If the addition does not disrupt your living spaces, you can usually stay. For kitchen expansions or work across several rooms, a temporary move may be more comfortable.

Can you match my existing flooring, trim and finishes?

In most cases, yes. We source matching materials when we can, or choose complementary finishes, and we settle this during design so there are no surprises.
