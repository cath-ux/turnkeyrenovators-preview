Board types

The right board for each room

Drywall is not one product. The room, the moisture and the noise decide which board goes on the wall.

Standard board

Lightweight half-inch drywall is easy to work with and is the usual choice for living rooms and bedrooms.

  • Half-inch thickness
  • Living rooms
  • Bedrooms

Moisture-resistant board

Green board and similar panels resist humidity and are commonly used in bathrooms and other rooms with elevated moisture.

  • Bathrooms
  • Kitchens
  • Laundry rooms

Sound-reducing board

Soundproof drywall cuts noise between rooms, which helps home offices, studios and bedrooms next to living space.

  • Home offices
  • Studios
  • Shared walls

Fire-rated board

Type X board has glass fibers mixed into the gypsum core so it resists fire longer, and it is used in fire-rated wall assemblies.

  • Glass-fiber core
  • Fire-rated assemblies
  • Layered for higher ratings

Humidity and flood

Drywall in a humid, flood-prone city

Gypsum softens with water and paper facing feeds mold, so the climate shapes how we install and repair drywall here.

Dry it fast

EPA says wet materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually will not grow mold. Past that, damaged board should come out.

Install in the right conditions

Drywall goes up best between 55 and 95 degrees indoors, and at 70 degrees and 70 percent humidity joint compound can take about 26 hours to dry.

Catch the early signs

Yellowed or bubbled paint, soft spots, and brown stains around windows or air conditioners point to moisture that needs attention now.

Better joints

Fiberglass mesh tape and well-sealed joints, edges and screw holes help keep humid air out of the board.

Sources: EPA Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home, and our drywall care guide for New Orleans humidity.

How it works

How we hang a wall

Careful measuring and flat, well-fastened sheets save time at the finishing stage and avoid cracks later.

  1. 01

    Measure and cut

    We measure the wall or ceiling, score the sheet along a straight edge, snap it clean and rasp the edge for a tight fit.

  2. 02

    Hang the top sheets

    Upper sheets go up first, held tight to the framing with a small gap at the top to prevent cracking.

  3. 03

    Hang the bottom sheets

    Lower sheets go in with a consistent gap at the floor, with seams staggered from the row above for strength.

  4. 04

    Fasten

    Screws go in every 12 to 16 inches along the studs, set just below the surface without tearing the paper.

  5. 05

    Cut openings

    Windows, doors and outlets are cut out, with extra screws around each opening.

  6. 06

    Tape and finish

    Seams and screws get three coats of joint compound, each dried fully before sanding, ready for primer and paint.

Questions

Drywall questions

Repairs, materials and moisture, sorted by topic.

Can you match the texture of my existing walls?

Yes. Finishing, sanding and texture matching are part of every repair, so the patch blends into the surrounding surface instead of standing out.

When should a drywall hole be handled by a pro?

Small holes are simple fills. Holes larger than about 6 inches need a new piece of board fastened to a wood backer inside the wall, then taped and finished so the patch disappears.

What causes nail pops and cracks?

Nail pops happen when fasteners work loose from the framing. Cracks often show at seams and around windows and doors because the house moves. Cracks wider than 1/8 inch are worth a closer look.

Which drywall goes in a bathroom?

Moisture-resistant board, such as green board, is the standard for bathrooms and other rooms with elevated humidity. Standard half-inch board suits living rooms and bedrooms.

Does wet drywall always need to be replaced?

Not always. If the board dries out within a day or two, mold usually does not take hold. Board that stays wet, turns soft or shows mold should be cut out and replaced after the cavity dries.

How does New Orleans humidity affect drywall?

Humid air can swell and soften drywall and supports mold on the paper facing. Keeping indoor humidity in check, sealing joints well and using moisture-resistant board in wet rooms all help walls last.
