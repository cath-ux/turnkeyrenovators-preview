What renovating in Gretna means

Gretna is the parish seat, runs its own Building Department and protects two historic districts. Exterior work here often needs more than a permit.

City of Gretna permits

Gretna is an incorporated city. Its Building Department handles permitting and inspections, and most applications start online through the MGO Connect portal. We set up and track the permits for you.

Two historic districts

In Mechanickham/Old Gretna and McDonoghville, exterior work visible from the street also needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic District Commission. We prepare that application with the permit.

Older building stock

The downtown historic district holds buildings dating from about 1845 to 1935, mostly creole cottages, shotgun houses and bungalows. Work there calls for matching materials and careful demolition.

Flood-aware building

The city sits at 0 feet behind the Mississippi River levee, and its own flood mitigation page calls flooding an ongoing concern. We plan drainage and moisture details into every job.

Sources: City of Gretna Building Department; Gretna Historic District Commission; Gretna Flood Mitigation; Gretna Historic District (Wikipedia); Gretna, Louisiana (Wikipedia).

All about Gretna

All About Gretna, LA

Across the Mississippi from uptown New Orleans, Gretna occupies part of the West Bank and has served as Jefferson Parish's seat since 1884. Visitors know it for a historic downtown made for walking, filled with Creole cottages and shotgun houses, plus small museums, a farmers market on Saturdays and a major music festival every October.

In Gretna's historic district, buildings date from about 1845 to 1935, and many nearby neighborhoods took shape after the 1958 opening of the Mississippi River Bridge. Because of that range, we look at each building's age, framing and plumbing before planning any renovation, from kitchens and whole homes to exteriors and commercial spaces.

Interesting Facts About Gretna, LA

  • Seat of the Parish Since 1884Since 1884, Gretna has been the center of Jefferson Parish government. For decades the parish used the 1907 courthouse on Huey P. Long Avenue, which became Gretna City Hall at a 1964 dedication.
  • Built From Three SettlementsThree communities came together to form today's Gretna: McDonoghville from 1815, Mechanikham from 1836 and Gretna from 1838, the last developed by a ferry company. Official incorporation as a city came in 1913.
  • A Historic Volunteer Fire CompanyOrganized in 1841, David Crockett Volunteer Fire Company No. 1 is thought to be the country's oldest volunteer fire company in continuous operation. The fire hall it built in 1859 is still standing.
  • A Sprawling Historic DistrictSince 1985 the Gretna historic district has been on the National Register of Historic Places. It spans roughly 130 acres and contains over 500 contributing buildings.

Things To Do in Gretna, LA

  • German-American Cultural CenterAt 519 Huey P. Long Avenue, this free museum covers the history of German immigrants in Louisiana. Hours run Wednesday through Saturday from 10 a.m. until 3 p.m.
  • Gretna Farmers MarketRain or shine on Saturday mornings, vendors pack the covered market building along Huey P. Long Avenue, selling local produce, baked goods, plants and prepared food, along with live music.
  • Louisiana State Fire MuseumThe Gretna Historical Society uses the 1859 David Crockett fire hall to show an 1876 Gould steam fire pumper and firefighting artifacts gathered from across Louisiana.
  • Gretna Heritage FestivalEvery October, this downtown festival fills the riverfront with multiple music stages, local food and rides.

Notable Residents of Gretna, LA

  • Mel OttBorn in Gretna in 1909, this Baseball Hall of Fame outfielder hit 511 home runs with the New York Giants. He is honored in Gretna at Mel Ott Park, which features his statue.
  • Frankie FordBest known for the 1959 rock and roll hit Sea Cruise, the singer was a Gretna native born in 1939 who passed away there in 2015.

Public Transportation in Gretna, LA

On weekdays, the W6 Gretna Local from Jefferson Parish Transit runs between downtown Gretna, Wilty Terminal plus the Algiers ferry terminal, and Wilty connects riders to other West Bank routes. Gretna reaches New Orleans by way of the Crescent City Connection and the Westbank Expressway, while Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport sits on the other side of the river in Kenner.

Who issues building permits in Gretna?

The City of Gretna Building Department. Gretna is incorporated, so permits and inspections go through the city, and most applications start on the MGO Connect portal with Gretna selected as the jurisdiction.
