What renovating in Baton Rouge means

Baton Rouge shares a consolidated government with East Baton Rouge Parish, gets heavy rain and has a real storm record. Here is how we plan around it.

City-parish permits

Baton Rouge and East Baton Rouge Parish share a consolidated city-parish government, and permits and inspections run through the Permits and Inspections Division of the Department of Development. We handle the permit process for your project.

One of the wettest US cities

Baton Rouge averages about 62 inches of rain a year and ranks among the wettest cities in the country, so we treat drainage, roofing and moisture control as core parts of every plan.

Roofs after Gustav

Hurricane Gustav in 2008 brought winds above 100 miles per hour, and many roofs in the Highland Road, Garden District and Goodwood areas took tree damage. We build roofs and siding for that kind of storm.

All About Baton Rouge, LA

Louisiana's capital, Baton Rouge, lies along the Mississippi River's east bank within East Baton Rouge Parish. With 227,470 people counted in the 2020 census, it ranks as the second most populous city in the state. Louisiana State University and Southern University both call it home, while the Capitol tower, museums and the Old State Capitol line the downtown riverfront.

Housing in Baton Rouge ranges from older houses close to downtown to newer subdivisions spread throughout the parish. That spread shapes how we approach every renovation here, whether we are updating a kitchen in an older home, refreshing the exterior of a newer build or reworking space for a local business. We start each project by looking at how the home or building is actually used.

Interesting Facts About Baton Rouge, LA

  • Named After a Red PoleFrench explorer Pierre Le Moyne d'Iberville recorded a red pole in 1699 standing on the river bluff, where it marked the boundary separating the hunting grounds of the Houma and Bayougoula peoples. The city's name comes from the French phrase le bâton rouge.
  • A Capitol Built Like a SkyscraperGovernor Huey P. Long saw the Art Deco Louisiana State Capitol finished in 1932. Its 34 floors reach 450 feet, and according to state sources no state capitol in the country stands taller.
  • Seat of Government Since the 1840sIn 1846, the legislature voted to shift the seat of state government to Baton Rouge from New Orleans. Until the present tower went up from 1929 to 1932, lawmakers met on the river bluff in the Gothic Revival Old State Capitol.
  • Home to Two FlagshipsBaton Rouge holds the main campuses of two flagship schools. Louisiana State University leads the LSU System, and Southern University leads the Southern University System, which is the only historically Black college system in the nation.

Things To Do in Baton Rouge, LA

  • Louisiana State CapitolTake a free tour of the downtown Louisiana State Capitol and see its Art Deco halls, its murals and the landscaped grounds, moving at whatever pace suits you.
  • USS Kidd Veterans MuseumOn South River Road, the USS Kidd Veterans Museum is the home of USS Kidd, a destroyer from World War II. Because the ship has been off site for restoration, look at the museum's website to confirm current access to the ship.
  • LSU Rural Life MuseumOff Essen Lane, the LSU Rural Life Museum spreads 32 historic outbuildings across 25 acres to show how rural Louisiana lived in the 1700s and 1800s. Visitors guide themselves every day, and reservations are not required.
  • Louisiana Art & Science MuseumA former railroad depot from 1925 on the downtown riverfront now houses the Louisiana Art & Science Museum. Inside, visitors find the Irene W. Pennington Planetarium, an Ancient Egypt gallery and galleries devoted to both art and science.

Notable Residents of Baton Rouge, LA

  • Randy JacksonBorn June 23, 1956, in Baton Rouge, he went on to work as a bassist and record producer and spent 2002 to 2013 as a longtime American Idol judge.
  • Shane WestIn 1978 this actor and musician was born in Baton Rouge under the name Shannon Bruce Snaith. He is recognized for the series Nikita as well as A Walk to Remember.

Public Transportation in Baton Rouge, LA

Bus service in Baton Rouge and Baker comes from the Capital Area Transit System (CATS), which runs fixed routes along with on-demand and paratransit rides. In the city, Interstate 12 starts at Interstate 10, while US 190 uses the Huey P. Long Bridge to cross the Mississippi. Baton Rouge Metropolitan Airport (BTR) is located roughly four miles north of downtown.
