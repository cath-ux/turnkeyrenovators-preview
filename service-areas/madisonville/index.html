What renovating in Madisonville means

Madisonville is a small incorporated town on the Tchefuncte River, low to the water and rich in older buildings. Here is how we plan around it.

An incorporated town

Madisonville is an incorporated town with its own Town Hall on Highway 22 West. Before work starts, we confirm with the town which permits and inspections your project needs.

All About Madisonville, LA

On the Tchefuncte River, near the spot where it flows into Lake Pontchartrain, sits Madisonville, a small St. Tammany Parish town built along the water. Its early settlers knew it as Coquille, the French word for shells, until 1811, when it took a new name honoring President James Madison. At the 2020 census, 850 people lived here.

Life in Madisonville is tied to the river, and the town took on floodwater during Hurricane Katrina in 2005 and again during Hurricane Isaac in 2012. That history guides how we plan renovations here, whether the project is a kitchen, a full home, an exterior or a local business, with water-tolerant materials and layouts matched to each building's age and plumbing.

Interesting Facts About Madisonville, LA

  • Named After a PresidentThe first French settlers called the place Coquille, or shells, after the shells that lined the river and lake. The town adopted the name Madisonville in 1811 to honor President James Madison.
  • The Parish's Oldest SettlementAccording to the Town of Madisonville, it is St. Tammany Parish's oldest permanent settlement, with roots in a 1783 Spanish land grant that gave settler Jean Baptiste Baham 1,000 arpents.
  • A River Port Before the Civil WarIn the years before the Civil War, Madisonville was a bustling river port. Bricks and other goods produced in Tchefuncte River towns were shipped downriver and over the lake to New Orleans.
  • Wooden Boat FestivalEvery October the Tchefuncte riverfront fills with more than one hundred classic boats for the Wooden Boat Festival, hosted by Maritime Museum Louisiana.

Things To Do in Madisonville, LA

  • Maritime Museum LouisianaAt 133 Mabel Drive, Maritime Museum Louisiana covers the area's river and boating past with exhibits, boat building and educational programs. Hours run Tuesday through Saturday.
  • Fairview-Riverside State ParkStretching across 99 acres beside the Tchefuncte River, Fairview-Riverside State Park is a spot to camp, crab, fish or paddle, with a playground, picnic tables and a nature trail a quarter-mile long.
  • Otis House MuseumWithin Fairview-Riverside State Park stands the Otis House, the 1880s home of a sawmill owner, which Frank Otis renovated during the 1930s. Guided tours run twice, 11 a.m. and 1 p.m., on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays.
  • Tchefuncte River LighthouseDating to 1837, the Tchefuncte River Lighthouse stands where the river meets the lake, so plan to see it from a boat. Maritime Museum Louisiana now manages it.

Public Transportation in Madisonville, LA

Parish residents can use STAR Transit, a call-ahead, curb-to-curb service that COAST operates together with St. Tammany Parish Government. Within town, LA 22 crosses the Tchefuncte River by swing bridge and intersects LA 21 at Main Street, while Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport handles commercial flights.
