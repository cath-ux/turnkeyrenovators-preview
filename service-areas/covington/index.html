What renovating in Covington means

Covington is the parish seat, runs its own building permit office and protects a historic core. Here is how we plan around it.

City building permits

Covington is an incorporated city with its own Building Permit Department, and the state's 2021 I-Codes apply. Inspections are requested one business day ahead, and we schedule them into the job.

Historic district review

The Division of St. John, the city's historic core, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and the city has its own Historical District Commission. We check early whether your project needs its review.

Commercial life safety

For commercial renovations, life safety and ADA codes are administered by the Office of the State Fire Marshal, alongside the city's building permit.

The work we do here

In Covington we highlight bathroom renovation, drywall installation, demolition and concrete work for patios and driveways, backed by our full residential and commercial divisions.

All about Covington

All About Covington, LA

Set at the meeting point of the Bogue Falaya and Tchefuncte rivers, roughly 30 miles northeast of New Orleans, Covington serves as the parish seat for St. Tammany Parish. John Wharton Collins established the town as Wharton in 1813, and it took the name Covington in 1816. This historic Northshore city had 11,564 residents in the 2020 census.

Around Covington's historic downtown, many older homes remain, while newer neighborhoods fill in toward the edges of town. Working across both, we plan each renovation around what the structure already has, keeping original walls and plumbing in mind in older houses, whether the project is a kitchen, an exterior refresh or space for a business.

Interesting Facts About Covington, LA

  • Laid Out on the Fourth of JulyOn July 4, 1813, John Wharton Collins, a merchant from New Orleans, laid out the town and called it Wharton. His design featured squares with lots in the center and alleys that residents still refer to as ox lots.
  • Renamed for a War of 1812 OfficerThe town got its new name in 1816, when the legislature granted its charter, honoring General Leonard Covington. He was an officer in the War of 1812 who suffered a mortal wound at the Battle of Crysler's Farm.
  • From Railroad to TrailIn 1992 the parish purchased an old Illinois Central Railroad corridor, which the Tammany Trace now follows. Paved for 31 miles, the trail connects Covington to Abita Springs, Mandeville, Lacombe and Slidell.
  • Riverside Park Dating to 1909Along the Bogue Falaya River, Bogue Falaya Park first opened in July 1909. The park appears on the National Register of Historic Places, and its 1915 pavilion still serves as a spot where the community gathers.

Things To Do in Covington, LA

  • Covington TrailheadBegin your visit at the Tammany Trace's Covington Trailhead, designed to look like an old railroad station and fitted with a clock tower, a bandstand and an amphitheater. Each Wednesday between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m., a farmers market sets up at the site.
  • Bogue Falaya Wayside ParkBogue Falaya Wayside Park, located where North New Hampshire Street ends, is a launch point for kayaks and paddleboards. You will also find a disc golf course, a playground and a paved loop beside the river.
  • Three Rivers Art FestivalEvery November, the free Covington Three Rivers Art Festival takes over downtown Covington, making it worth building a weekend around.
  • Covington Farmers MarketEvery Saturday, 8 a.m. until noon, local growers and food vendors gather rain or shine for the Covington Farmers Market at 609 North Columbia Street, home of Covington City Hall.

Notable Residents of Covington, LA

  • Walker PercyHis debut novel The Moviegoer earned the National Book Award, and he made Covington his home, writing there for decades until he died at his house in Covington in 1990.
  • Ian SomerhalderKnown for his roles in Lost and The Vampire Diaries, the actor was born in 1978 in Covington, where he was raised.

Public Transportation in Covington, LA

St. Tammany Parish residents can book curb-to-curb rides in advance through STAR Transit. Covington is served by both Interstate 12 and US 190, and US 190 heads south to the Lake Pontchartrain Causeway's north end. For commercial flights, travelers rely on Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport.
